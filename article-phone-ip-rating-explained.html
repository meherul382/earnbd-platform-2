IP Ratings on Phones Explained: What IP67 and IP68 Mean

IP ratings describe protection against solid particles and water under defined test conditions. They are useful, but they are easy to misunderstand.

An IP rating should be treated as a design feature and safety margin, not as permission to intentionally expose a phone to water.

What an IP rating means

The first part of an IP code relates to protection from solids and the second part relates to water. The exact test conditions matter.

Why IP67 and IP68 are not identical

Different ratings use different test conditions, and manufacturers may publish additional limits for their models. Check the exact device documentation.

Resistance can change

Seals can age and drops or repairs can affect protection. Do not assume a damaged phone has the same resistance as a new one.

Be careful with liquids

Salt water, drinks and dirty water can create different risks from controlled testing. If a phone gets wet, follow manufacturer guidance instead of deliberately testing it.

Protect the charging port

Do not connect a cable to a wet port. Let the device dry according to official guidance and avoid forcing objects into the connector.

FAQ

Does IP68 mean waterproof?

No. It describes protection under defined test conditions rather than unlimited waterproofing.

Can a cracked phone keep its resistance?

You should not assume damaged seals maintain the original protection.

Bottom line: An IP rating can reduce risk from accidental exposure, but careful handling is still the best protection.

Editorial note: Exact resistance conditions and warranty terms vary by device manufacturer.
