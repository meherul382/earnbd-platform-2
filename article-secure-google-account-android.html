How to Secure Your Google Account on Android

A practical guide to protecting a Google account on Android with passkeys, two-step verification, recovery options, and phishing awareness.

Your Google Account may connect email, photos, contacts, app purchases, backups, and other personal services. Protecting it is therefore an important part of using an Android phone safely. No single setting prevents every attack, but a few deliberate steps can make unauthorized access more difficult and help you recover if something goes wrong.

1. Use a unique, strong sign-in method

A password reused on multiple websites creates risk: if one service is breached, attackers may try the same password elsewhere. Use a long, unique password for your Google Account. A reputable password manager can help you create and store it. Where supported and suitable for your situation, consider a passkey, which uses your device's screen lock or another supported authentication method instead of asking you to type a password.

2. Turn on two-step verification

Two-step verification adds another check after your password. Open your Google Account security settings and review the available options. Google prompts, authenticator apps, passkeys, and security keys may be available depending on your account and device. Prefer methods that are resistant to phishing when practical. Keep in mind that no method is completely risk-free, and your recovery plan still matters.

3. Review recovery information

Check that your recovery phone number and recovery email are current and controlled by you. Recovery details can help you regain access if you forget your password or lose a device. Do not use an email address you can no longer access. Store backup codes securely if Google provides them for your account, and never share them with someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

4. Check devices and recent security activity

In your Google Account's security area, review devices where you are signed in and recent security events. Sign out of devices you no longer own or recognize. If an unfamiliar sign-in appears, change your password from a trusted device, review recovery information, and remove suspicious access. A location or device label can sometimes be approximate, so investigate context rather than assuming every unfamiliar label proves an intrusion.

5. Be careful with links, attachments, and verification requests

Phishing messages may imitate Google, banks, delivery services, or people you know. They may create urgency and ask you to sign in through a link, download a file, or share a code. When a message claims there is an account problem, open the official app or type the service's address yourself instead of following an unexpected link. Google will not need you to tell a stranger your password or one-time verification code.

6. Review third-party app access

Some apps and websites can request access to parts of your Google Account. Review connected apps and remove access you no longer need or do not recognize. Be cautious about granting broad permissions to services with unclear ownership or purpose. Removing access may stop an app from working, so check before disconnecting something you rely on.

7. Secure your Android phone itself

Use a strong screen lock, install Android and app updates from trusted sources, and avoid installing apps from unknown websites. Review app permissions, especially access to accessibility services, notifications, SMS, microphone, camera, and files. These permissions can be legitimate for some apps, but unexpected requests deserve attention. Enable the device-finding and remote security features available for your phone, and keep the device's recovery options up to date.

8. Protect your account on shared or borrowed devices

Avoid saving passwords on public or borrowed devices. If you must sign in, use a private session where appropriate, sign out afterward, and do not approve unexpected sign-in prompts. On a device you do not control, avoid opening sensitive files or changing security settings unless necessary.

What to do if you suspect someone accessed your account

  1. Use a trusted device and visit your Google Account security settings directly.
  2. Change your password if you believe it has been exposed.
  3. Review signed-in devices and sign out of unfamiliar sessions.
  4. Check recovery information and remove changes you did not make.
  5. Review recent security events and third-party access.
  6. Follow Google's official account-recovery guidance if you cannot sign in.

If the account is used for financial, business, or sensitive work, also check connected services and notify relevant administrators or providers when appropriate.

Account security checklist

  • Use a unique password or an appropriate passkey.
  • Enable two-step verification.
  • Keep recovery information current.
  • Review devices and security activity periodically.
  • Remove unnecessary third-party access.
  • Install trusted updates and use a secure screen lock.
  • Never share passwords, backup codes, or verification codes.

Bottom line: Account security is an ongoing habit, not a one-time setup. A unique sign-in method, additional verification, current recovery details, and careful handling of unexpected requests provide a practical foundation for protecting your Google Account on Android.

Editorial note: Menu names and available features can vary by phone model, Android version, region, and service settings. Check your device manufacturer's documentation for model-specific instructions.
