QR Code Safety: How to Scan Links Without Unnecessary Risks

QR codes are convenient because they turn a physical sign or message into a web link. The destination may not be obvious until you scan it.

Treat a QR code like any other link: inspect the destination, be cautious with unexpected requests and never enter sensitive information simply because a page asks for it.

Inspect the destination

When your phone shows the link before opening it, read the domain carefully. Watch for misspellings, extra words or addresses that do not match the organization you expect.

Be careful with login requests

For important accounts, consider opening the official app or website yourself instead of entering credentials into an unexpected page. Never share one-time authentication codes.

Pause before paying

Review the recipient and amount inside a trusted payment application before confirming a transaction. Urgency and prize claims are reasons to verify first.

Keep software current

Use current browsers and official app stores. Avoid unknown scanner apps that request unnecessary permissions.

FAQ

Is scanning itself the main risk?

The bigger risk is what you do after opening the destination, such as entering credentials or approving a payment.

What if I already entered a password?

Change it through the legitimate service and review account security as soon as possible.

Bottom line: A QR code is only a shortcut to information. Verify the destination before trusting the request.

Editorial note: Security practices and service interfaces can change over time.
