How to Check Whether a Used Smartphone Is Safe to Buy

A practical second-hand phone inspection checklist covering ownership, locks, hardware, battery, connectivity, and payment safety.

A used smartphone can offer good value, but a low price is not enough reason to buy. Before paying, check that the seller can transfer the phone properly, the device works as expected, and it is not locked, damaged, or reported lost. Take your time and test the phone in person whenever possible.

1. Ask about ownership and proof of purchase

Ask why the phone is being sold and whether the seller has a receipt, original order record, or other reasonable proof of ownership. Compare the device details on the phone with the box or receipt if available. A box alone does not prove ownership because boxes and devices can be separated. Avoid a transaction if the seller refuses basic questions or pressures you to pay immediately.

2. Check account locks before resetting

Make sure the seller removes their accounts and disables any device-finding or activation lock features using the official process. On Android, Factory Reset Protection may require the previously synced Google account after a reset. On an iPhone, Activation Lock can prevent a new owner from activating the device. Ask the seller to complete the proper sign-out and reset process, then verify that the phone reaches its normal setup screen and can be activated. Do not rely on promises that a lock can be bypassed later.

3. Inspect the body and screen

Look closely for cracks, deep dents, lifted screen edges, gaps, corrosion, or signs that the phone has been opened poorly. Test the touchscreen across the entire display by typing and dragging an icon around the screen. Check for dead pixels, unusual color patches, flicker, and burn-in. Test the physical buttons, vibration, speakers, microphones, and cameras.

4. Test battery condition and charging

Battery-health information differs by model and may not be available on every Android phone. Check the manufacturer's battery menu or diagnostics if provided. Observe whether the battery percentage drops unusually quickly during a short test, whether the phone shuts down unexpectedly, or whether it becomes excessively hot. Connect a compatible charger and confirm that charging begins reliably. Do not buy a phone with a swollen battery or visible battery damage.

5. Test calls, mobile data, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth

Insert your own SIM if practical and check that the phone recognizes it. Make a brief call, test the microphone and earpiece, and confirm mobile data works. Connect to a Wi-Fi network and, if relevant, pair a Bluetooth accessory. Confirm that the phone supports the network bands and SIM configuration you need in your area. A device can be genuine yet still lack compatibility with a particular carrier.

6. Verify the device identity

Find the IMEI or serial number in the phone's settings or using the manufacturer's documented method. Compare it with the number on the device packaging or purchase documentation when available. Use your carrier or the relevant official national device-check service, where one exists, to check status. Availability and accuracy of IMEI checks vary by country. A clean result is useful but does not guarantee that a device will never later be reported lost or blocked.

7. Check cameras, storage, and sensors

Take photos with each rear camera mode and the front camera. Record a short video and play it back with sound. Test the flashlight, proximity sensor during a call if possible, auto-rotate, fingerprint reader, and face unlock if offered. Open storage settings to confirm the advertised capacity. Check for warning messages, unexpected restarts, or apps that you cannot remove because of unusual management restrictions.

8. Review software and update support

Check the installed operating-system version and whether the manufacturer still provides security updates for that model. Older phones may run current apps but no longer receive security patches. Review the phone's update settings and look up the manufacturer's support information for the exact model and region. Avoid devices with unknown firmware or unexplained bootloader warnings unless you understand the implications.

9. Make payment safely

Whenever possible, meet in a safe public location and complete inspection before payment. Use a payment method that provides a clear record. Write down the model, serial or IMEI, price, date, and seller details on a simple receipt if appropriate. Do not share account passwords or one-time verification codes with the seller. Be cautious of unusually low prices, advance-payment demands, and sellers who will not let you test the phone.

Used-phone inspection checklist

  • Ownership questions answered and documentation reviewed.
  • Previous account and activation locks properly removed.
  • Screen, frame, buttons, speakers, and microphones tested.
  • Battery and charging behavior checked.
  • SIM, calls, mobile data, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth tested.
  • IMEI or serial information compared and checked where possible.
  • Cameras, sensors, storage, and software status reviewed.
  • Payment and receipt handled safely.

Bottom line: A careful inspection reduces risk but cannot eliminate it. If you cannot verify ownership, remove account locks through official steps, or test essential functions, walk away rather than assuming the issue can be fixed later.

Editorial note: Menu names and available features can vary by phone model, Android version, region, and service settings. Check your device manufacturer's documentation for model-specific instructions.
